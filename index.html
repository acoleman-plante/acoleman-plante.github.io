About me

I graduated from CU Boulder in 2024 with a degree in astronomy (and a minor in French). Before beginning graduate school at Yale, I spent two years as a post-baccalaureate fellow at the National Radio Astronomy Observatory.

My research focuses on low-mass star formation using observations from radio interferometers like ALMA and VLA, as well as JWST. I am also passionate about planet formation and understanding the chemical and physical ingredients for life in protostellar and protoplanetary disks.

Research Projects

Investigating the HD 139664 Debris Disk and Star with ALMA

In this project, I used an MCMC framework to model the architecture of the HD 139664 debris disk, which we found to be unusually broad and located surprisingly close to the star. We also detected the first-ever millimeter flares from an F-type star. This work was published in the University of Colorado Honors Journal, which you can find here.

Multiplicity in the Early Stages of Star Formation: The Taurus Molecular Cloud

In this study, we used ALMA and VLA observations to measure the multiplicity fraction and separation distribution of protostars in Taurus, comparing the results to denser star-forming regions like Orion and Perseus, as well as to a more evolved Class II/III population in Taurus. We find that Taurus shows an elevated level of multiplicity relative to other regions, but consistent with the more evolved Taurus population..

Weighing Young Stars in the Taurus Molecular Cloud (in progress)

This project uses ALMA observations of 13CO and C18O to model the Keplerian rotation of gas around the same protostars analyzed in the multiplicity survey. Using radiative-transfer modeling, we measure dynamical masses for the central protostars and compare these with evolutionary stage, luminosity, and multiplicity.

Characterizing Protostellar Accretion in Orion with JWST (in progress)

In this project, we use JWST NIRSpec and MIRI MRS IFU data from the HEFE Large Program to characterize accretion in 14 protostars in Orion. By extracting hydrogen recombination lines that trace accretion shocks, we separate the contributions of intrinsic stellar luminosity and accretion luminosity to better understand how mass is assembled in the earliest stages of star formation.
